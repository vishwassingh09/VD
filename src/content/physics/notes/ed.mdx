### Topic 1: Coulomb's Law, Electric Field & Gauss's Law

<div class="ed-theory-box">

**Short Theory (Notes Ch. 2 — Electrostatics):**

For a source charge $q_i$ at $\vec{r}_i'$ and a test charge $Q$ at field point $\vec{r}$, define the separation vector $\vec{\mathscr{r}}_i = \vec{r} - \vec{r}_i'$. By superposition:

$$
\vec{E}(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{q_i}{\mathscr{r}_i^2}\hat{\mathscr{r}}_i = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int \frac{dq}{\mathscr{r}^2}\hat{\mathscr{r}}
$$

where $dq = \lambda\,dl'$ (line), $\sigma\,da'$ (surface), or $\rho\,d\tau'$ (volume). Using spherical symmetry and the Divergence Theorem ($\nabla\cdot(\hat{r}/r^2) = 4\pi\delta^3(\vec{r})$) gives **Gauss's Law** and the **Curl of $\vec{E}$**:

$$
\oint_S \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{a} = \frac{Q_{\text{enc}}}{\epsilon_0}, \qquad \vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{E} = \frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}, \qquad \vec{\nabla}\times\vec{E} = 0
$$

</div>

<ProblemCard num="1" title="Electric Field on the Axis of a Charged Circular Loop" tag="Griffiths Ch. 2 · Continuous Charge">
  <div slot="problem">
    Find the electric field $\vec{E}$ at a point $P$ at distance $z$ directly above the center of a thin circular ring of radius $R$ carrying a uniform linear charge density $\lambda$.
  </div>

  <div slot="answer">
    $$
    \vec{E}(z) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{2\pi R\lambda\, z}{(R^2 + z^2)^{3/2}}\,\hat{z} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q_{\text{tot}}\, z}{(R^2 + z^2)^{3/2}}\,\hat{z}
    $$
  </div>

  <div slot="solution">
    Take a line element $dl' = R\,d\phi'$ on the ring at $\vec{r}' = R\hat{s}'$, with field point $\vec{r} = z\hat{z}$. Then:

    $$
    \vec{\mathscr{r}} = \vec{r} - \vec{r}' = z\hat{z} - R\hat{s}', \qquad \mathscr{r} = \sqrt{R^2 + z^2}
    $$

    By azimuthal symmetry around the $z$-axis, all horizontal ($\hat{s}'$) components cancel when integrating $\phi'$ from $0$ to $2\pi$. Only the $\hat{z}$ component survives:

    $$
    E_z = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int_0^{2\pi} \frac{\lambda R\,d\phi'}{\mathscr{r}^2}\cos\theta, \qquad \cos\theta = \frac{z}{\sqrt{R^2 + z^2}}
    $$

    Since $R$, $z$, and $\mathscr{r}$ are constant along the ring:

    $$
    \vec{E}(z) = \frac{\lambda R z}{4\pi\epsilon_0(R^2 + z^2)^{3/2}}\left(\int_0^{2\pi} d\phi'\right)\hat{z} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{2\pi R\lambda\, z}{(R^2 + z^2)^{3/2}}\,\hat{z}
    $$
  </div>
</ProblemCard>

### Topic 2: Electric Potential & Electrostatic Boundary Conditions

<div class="ed-theory-box">

**Short Theory (Notes Ch. 2 — Potential & Boundary Conditions):**

Because $\vec{\nabla}\times\vec{E} = 0$, the line integral is path-independent and defines the scalar potential $V(\vec{r}) = -\int_{\mathcal{O}}^{\vec{r}} \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{l}$, so $\vec{E} = -\vec{\nabla}V$ and $\nabla^2 V = -\rho/\epsilon_0$ (Poisson's equation).

Integrating $\vec{E}$ across a wafer-thin Gaussian pillbox ($\epsilon \to 0$) on a surface with charge density $\sigma$ yields the **boundary conditions**:

$$
E_{\text{above}}^\perp - E_{\text{below}}^\perp = \frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0}, \qquad \vec{E}_{\text{above}}^\parallel = \vec{E}_{\text{below}}^\parallel
$$

$$
V_{\text{above}} = V_{\text{below}}, \qquad \frac{\partial V_{\text{above}}}{\partial n} - \frac{\partial V_{\text{below}}}{\partial n} = -\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0}
$$

</div>

<ProblemCard num="2" title="Potential & Surface Charge from Boundary Conditions" tag="Griffiths Ch. 2 · Boundary Conditions">
  <div slot="problem">
    The electric potential in a region containing a flat sheet of charge at $z = 0$ is given by:

    $$
    V(z) = \begin{cases} V_0\,e^{-\kappa z}, & z > 0 \\ V_0\,(1 + \alpha z), & z < 0 \end{cases}
    $$

    where $V_0$, $\kappa$, and $\alpha$ are positive constants. Find the surface charge density $\sigma$ on the plane $z = 0$.
  </div>

  <div slot="answer">
    $$
    \sigma = \epsilon_0 V_0 (\kappa + \alpha)
    $$
  </div>

  <div slot="solution">
    First note that at $z = 0$, $V_{\text{above}}(0^+) = V_0 = V_{\text{below}}(0^-)$, so $V$ is continuous as required.

    With normal $\hat{n} = \hat{z}$, the normal derivatives are:

    $$
    \left.\frac{\partial V_{\text{above}}}{\partial z}\right|_{z=0^+} = -\kappa V_0, \qquad \left.\frac{\partial V_{\text{below}}}{\partial z}\right|_{z=0^-} = \alpha V_0
    $$

    Apply the potential boundary condition $\frac{\partial V_{\text{above}}}{\partial n} - \frac{\partial V_{\text{below}}}{\partial n} = -\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0}$:

    $$
    (-\kappa V_0) - (\alpha V_0) = -\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0} \implies \sigma = \epsilon_0 V_0 (\kappa + \alpha)
    $$
  </div>
</ProblemCard>

### Topic 3: Work & Energy in Electrostatics

<div class="ed-theory-box">

**Short Theory (Notes Ch. 2 — Electrostatic Energy):**

Potential is the work per unit charge needed to assemble the system. For discrete and continuous charge distributions:

$$
W = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^n q_i V(\vec{r}_i) = \frac{1}{2}\int \rho V\,d\tau
$$

Substituting $\rho = \epsilon_0 \vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{E}$, integrating by parts, and letting the boundary surface go to infinity gives the field energy density over all space:

$$
W = \frac{\epsilon_0}{2}\int_{\text{all space}} E^2\,d\tau
$$

</div>

<ProblemCard num="3" title="Total Electrostatic Energy of a Uniformly Charged Solid Sphere" tag="Griffiths Ch. 2 · Field Energy">
  <div slot="problem">
    A solid insulating sphere of radius $R$ carries a total charge $Q$ uniformly distributed throughout its volume. Compute the total electrostatic energy $W$ required to assemble it using $W = \frac{\epsilon_0}{2}\int_{\text{all space}} E^2\,d\tau$.
  </div>

  <div slot="answer">
    $$
    W = \frac{3}{5}\,\frac{Q^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 R}
    $$
  </div>

  <div slot="solution">
    By Gauss's law, the electric field inside and outside the sphere is:

    $$
    \vec{E}(r) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{Q r}{4\pi\epsilon_0 R^3}\,\hat{r}, & r < R \\[8pt] \dfrac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r^2}\,\hat{r}, & r > R \end{cases}
    $$

    Split the all-space integral ($d\tau = 4\pi r^2\,dr$) into $0 \to R$ and $R \to \infty$:

    $$
    W = \frac{\epsilon_0}{2}\left[\int_0^R \left(\frac{Q r}{4\pi\epsilon_0 R^3}\right)^2 4\pi r^2\,dr + \int_R^\infty \left(\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r^2}\right)^2 4\pi r^2\,dr\right]
    $$

    $$
    W = \frac{Q^2}{8\pi\epsilon_0}\left[\frac{1}{R^6}\int_0^R r^4\,dr + \int_R^\infty \frac{dr}{r^2}\right] = \frac{Q^2}{8\pi\epsilon_0}\left(\frac{1}{5R} + \frac{1}{R}\right) = \frac{3}{5}\frac{Q^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 R}
    $$
  </div>
</ProblemCard>

### Topic 4: Uniqueness Theorems & Method of Images

<div class="ed-theory-box">

**Short Theory (Notes Ch. 3 — Potentials & Uniqueness):**

- **First Uniqueness Theorem:** In a volume $V$ with charge density $\rho$, the solution to Poisson's equation $\nabla^2 V = -\rho/\epsilon_0$ is uniquely determined if $V$ is specified on all boundary surfaces $S$.
- **Second Uniqueness Theorem:** In a volume $V$ surrounded by conductors containing specified $\rho$ in the space between them, $\vec{E}$ is uniquely determined if the *total charge* $Q_i$ on each conductor is given.
- **Method of Images:** If an auxiliary charge configuration outside the region of interest produces the *exact same boundary conditions* on $S$ and the same $\rho$ inside $V$, Uniqueness guarantees it gives the exact potential $V$ inside $V$.

</div>

<ProblemCard num="4" title="Point Charge Above a Grounded Conducting Plane" tag="Griffiths Ch. 3 · Method of Images">
  <div slot="problem">
    A point charge $+q$ is held at distance $d$ above an infinite, grounded ($V = 0$) conducting plane at $z = 0$.
    1. Find the induced surface charge density $\sigma(x, y)$ on the conducting plane.
    2. Find the force on the charge $+q$.
  </div>

  <div slot="answer">
    1. $\sigma(x, y) = -\dfrac{qd}{2\pi (x^2 + y^2 + d^2)^{3/2}}$
    2. $\vec{F} = -\dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{q^2}{(2d)^2}\,\hat{z}$ (attractive toward the plane)
  </div>

  <div slot="solution">
    Place an image charge $-q$ at $(0, 0, -d)$. For $z \ge 0$, the potential is:

    $$
    V(x, y, z) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left[\frac{q}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + (z - d)^2}} - \frac{q}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + (z + d)^2}}\right]
    $$

    At $z = 0$, $V = 0$, and $V \to 0$ as $r \to \infty$, matching the boundary conditions for $z \ge 0$.

    **1. Induced surface charge:** Using $\sigma = -\epsilon_0 \left.\frac{\partial V}{\partial z}\right|_{z=0^+}$:

    $$
    \left.\frac{\partial V}{\partial z}\right|_{z=0^+} = \frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left[\frac{d}{(x^2+y^2+d^2)^{3/2}} + \frac{d}{(x^2+y^2+d^2)^{3/2}}\right] = \frac{qd}{2\pi\epsilon_0(x^2+y^2+d^2)^{3/2}}
    $$

    Hence $\sigma(x,y) = -\dfrac{qd}{2\pi(x^2+y^2+d^2)^{3/2}}$. (Integrating over the entire $xy$-plane gives $Q_{\text{ind}} = -q$.)

    **2. Force on $+q$:** Since the field at $(0,0,d)$ from the induced surface charges equals the field of the image charge $-q$ at $(0,0,-d)$ separated by distance $2d$:

    $$
    \vec{F} = -\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q^2}{(2d)^2}\,\hat{z}
    $$
  </div>
</ProblemCard>

### Topic 5: Separation of Variables (Cartesian)

<div class="ed-theory-box">

**Short Theory (Notes Ch. 3 — Separation of Variables):**

To solve Laplace's equation $\frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial y^2} = 0$ in a semi-infinite 2D slot ($y \in [0, a]$, $x \ge 0$) with $V(x,0) = V(x,a) = 0$, $V(\infty,y) \to 0$, and $V(0,y) = V_0(y)$, try $V(x,y) = X(x)Y(y)$:

$$
\frac{1}{X}\frac{d^2 X}{dx^2} + \frac{1}{Y}\frac{d^2 Y}{dy^2} = 0 \implies X'' = k^2 X, \quad Y'' = -k^2 Y
$$

Applying the boundary conditions gives $k = n\pi/a$ and the Fourier sine series solution:

$$
V(x, y) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty C_n\,e^{-n\pi x/a}\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right), \qquad C_n = \frac{2}{a}\int_0^a V_0(y)\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right)dy
$$

</div>

  name="separation-slot"
  caption="Semi-infinite 2D conducting slot with V = 0 on top/bottom plates and V₀(y) at x = 0 (from notes p. 10)."
/>

<ProblemCard num="5" title="Potential in a 2D Slot with Constant End Potential" tag="Griffiths Ch. 3 · Separation of Variables">
  <div slot="problem">
    In the semi-infinite slot shown above, the end strip at $x = 0$ is held at a constant potential $V_0(y) = V_0$. Find the Fourier coefficients $C_n$ and write the series solution for $V(x, y)$.
  </div>

  <div slot="answer">
    $$
    V(x, y) = \frac{4V_0}{\pi} \sum_{n=1,3,5,\dots}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n}\,e^{-n\pi x/a}\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right)
    $$
  </div>

  <div slot="solution">
    Evaluate $C_n$ from Fourier orthogonality:

    $$
    C_n = \frac{2}{a}\int_0^a V_0 \sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right)dy = \frac{2V_0}{a}\left[-\frac{a}{n\pi}\cos\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right)\right]_0^a = \frac{2V_0}{n\pi}\left[1 - (-1)^n\right]
    $$

    Thus $C_n = 0$ for even $n$, and $C_n = \frac{4V_0}{n\pi}$ for odd $n = 1, 3, 5, \dots$, giving:

    $$
    V(x, y) = \frac{4V_0}{\pi} \sum_{n=1,3,5,\dots}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n}\,e^{-n\pi x/a}\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right)
    $$

    *(Closed form from notes p. 10: $V(x, y) = \frac{2V_0}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{\sin(\pi y/a)}{\sinh(\pi x/a)}\right)$.)*
  </div>
</ProblemCard>

### Topic 6: Multipole Expansion

<div class="ed-theory-box">

**Short Theory (Notes Ch. 3 — Multipole Expansion):**

For a localized charge distribution $\rho(\vec{r}')$, expand $\frac{1}{\mathscr{r}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{r^2 + (r')^2 - 2rr'\cos\alpha}}$ in powers of $r'/r$ using Legendre polynomials $P_n(\cos\alpha)$:

$$
V(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{r^{n+1}} \int (r')^n P_n(\cos\alpha)\,\rho(\vec{r}')\,d\tau'
$$

- $n = 0$ (Monopole, $\sim 1/r$): $Q = \int \rho\,d\tau'$
- $n = 1$ (Dipole, $\sim 1/r^2$): $\vec{p} = \int \vec{r}'\rho(\vec{r}')\,d\tau'$, giving $V_{\text{dip}} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\vec{p}\cdot\hat{r}}{r^2}$
- $n = 2$ (Quadrupole, $\sim 1/r^3$)

</div>

<EdDiagram
  name="multipole-geometry"

<ProblemCard num="6" title="Monopole and Dipole Terms of Two Opposite Charges" tag="Griffiths Ch. 3 · Multipole Expansion">
  <div slot="problem">
    A charge $+q$ is placed at $(0, 0, +a)$ and a charge $-q$ is placed at $(0, 0, -a)$. Find the monopole and dipole terms in the multipole expansion of $V(r, \theta)$ for $r \gg a$.
  </div>

  <div slot="answer">
    Monopole term: $V_{\text{mono}} = 0$. Dipole moment: $\vec{p} = 2qa\,\hat{z}$, giving:
    $$
    V_{\text{dip}}(r, \theta) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{2qa\cos\theta}{r^2}
    $$
  </div>

  <div slot="solution">
    1. **Monopole ($n = 0$):** Total charge $Q_{\text{tot}} = (+q) + (-q) = 0$, so $V_{\text{mono}} = 0$.
    2. **Dipole ($n = 1$):** Compute the dipole moment $\vec{p} = \sum q_i \vec{r}_i'$:

    $$
    \vec{p} = (+q)(+a\hat{z}) + (-q)(-a\hat{z}) = 2qa\,\hat{z}
    $$

    Since $\hat{z}\cdot\hat{r} = \cos\theta$, the leading far-field potential is:

    $$
    V(r, \theta) \approx \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\vec{p}\cdot\hat{r}}{r^2} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{2qa\cos\theta}{r^2}
    $$
  </div>
</ProblemCard>
