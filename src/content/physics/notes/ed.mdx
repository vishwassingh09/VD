### 1. Coulomb's Law, Electric Field & Gauss's Law

<div class="ed-theory-box">

**Short Theory (Notes Ch. 2 — Electrostatics):**

For source charges $\{(q_i, \vec{r}_i')\}$ and a test charge $Q$ at field point $\vec{r}$, the separation vector is $\vec{\mathscr{r}}_i = \vec{r} - \vec{r}_i'$. The electric field is:

$$
\vec{E}(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{q_i}{\mathscr{r}_i^2}\hat{\mathscr{r}}_i = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int \frac{dq}{\mathscr{r}^2}\hat{\mathscr{r}}
$$

where $dq = \lambda\,dl'$ (line), $\sigma\,da'$ (surface), or $\rho\,d\tau'$ (volume). Using spherical symmetry and $\nabla\cdot(\hat{r}/r^2) = 4\pi\delta^3(\vec{r})$ yields **Gauss's Law** and the **Curl of $\vec{E}$**:

$$
\oint_S \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{a} = \frac{Q_{\text{enc}}}{\epsilon_0}, \qquad \vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{E} = \frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}, \qquad \vec{\nabla}\times\vec{E} = 0
$$

</div>

  name="coulomb-vectors"

<ProblemCard num="2.5" title="Electric Field Above a Charged Circular Loop" tag="Griffiths Ch. 2 · Problem 2.5">
  <div slot="problem">
    Find the electric field a distance $z$ above the center of a circular loop of radius $r$ that carries a uniform line charge $\lambda$.
  </div>

  <div slot="answer">
    $$
    \vec{E}(z) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{2\pi r \lambda z}{(r^2 + z^2)^{3/2}}\,\hat{z}
    $$
  </div>

  <div slot="solution">
    By symmetry, the horizontal components of the field from opposite line elements cancel, leaving only the $z$-component. For each infinitesimal segment $dl' = r\,d\phi'$:

    $$
    dE_z = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\lambda\,dl'}{\mathscr{r}^2}\cos\theta, \qquad \mathscr{r} = \sqrt{r^2 + z^2}, \qquad \cos\theta = \frac{z}{\sqrt{r^2 + z^2}}
    $$

    Since $\mathscr{r}$ and $\theta$ are constant around the entire loop ($\int dl' = 2\pi r$):

    $$
    E_z = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\lambda z}{(r^2 + z^2)^{3/2}}\int_0^{2\pi} r\,d\phi' = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{2\pi r \lambda z}{(r^2 + z^2)^{3/2}}
    $$

    Therefore $\vec{E}(z) = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{2\pi r \lambda z}{(r^2 + z^2)^{3/2}}\,\hat{z}$.
  </div>
</ProblemCard>

### 2. Electric Potential & Boundary Conditions

<div class="ed-theory-box">

**Short Theory (Notes Ch. 2 — Potential & Boundary Conditions):**

Since $\vec{\nabla}\times\vec{E} = 0$, we define $V(\vec{r}) = -\int_{\mathcal{O}}^{\vec{r}} \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{l}$, so $\vec{E} = -\vec{\nabla}V$ and $\nabla^2 V = -\rho/\epsilon_0$ (Poisson's equation). Integrating across a wafer-thin Gaussian pillbox ($\epsilon \to 0$) on a sheet of surface charge $\sigma$ gives:

$$
E_{\text{above}}^\perp - E_{\text{below}}^\perp = \frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0}, \qquad \vec{E}_{\text{above}}^\parallel = \vec{E}_{\text{below}}^\parallel
$$

$$
V_{\text{above}} = V_{\text{below}}, \qquad \frac{\partial V_{\text{above}}}{\partial n} - \frac{\partial V_{\text{below}}}{\partial n} = -\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0}
$$

</div>

  name="gaussian-pillbox"

/>

<ProblemCard num="2.30" title="Cylindrical Shell Boundary Condition Check" tag="Griffiths Ch. 2 · Problem 2.30 (b)–(c)">
  <div slot="problem">
    Use Gauss's law to find the electric field inside and outside a long hollow cylindrical tube of radius $R$ carrying a uniform surface charge $\sigma$.
    1. Check that the field jump across the surface matches $\vec{E}_{\text{out}} - \vec{E}_{\text{in}} = \dfrac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0}\hat{s}$.
    2. Using the potential $V(r) = -\dfrac{R\sigma}{\epsilon_0}\ln(r/R)$ outside ($r > R$) and $V = 0$ inside ($r < R$), verify that $V$ is continuous at $r = R$ and $\dfrac{\partial V_{\text{out}}}{\partial r} - \dfrac{\partial V_{\text{in}}}{\partial r} = -\dfrac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0}$.
  </div>

  <div slot="answer">
    $\vec{E}_{\text{in}} = \mathbf{0}$, $\vec{E}_{\text{out}} = \dfrac{\sigma R}{\epsilon_0 s}\hat{s}$, so at $s = R$, $\Delta\vec{E} = \dfrac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0}\hat{s}$ and $\Delta\left(\dfrac{\partial V}{\partial r}\right) = -\dfrac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0}$.
  </div>

  <div slot="solution">
    **1. Electric Field via Gauss's Law:** Choose a coaxial cylindrical Gaussian surface of radius $s$ and length $l$:
    - **Outside ($s > R$):** $Q_{\text{enc}} = \sigma(2\pi R l)$, so:
      $$
      \oint \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{a} = E(2\pi s l) = \frac{\sigma(2\pi R l)}{\epsilon_0} \implies \vec{E}_{\text{out}} = \frac{\sigma R}{\epsilon_0 s}\,\hat{s}
      $$
      At the surface ($s = R$), $\vec{E}_{\text{out}} = \dfrac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0}\hat{s}$.
    - **Inside ($s < R$):** $Q_{\text{enc}} = 0 \implies \vec{E}_{\text{in}} = \mathbf{0}$.
    - **Jump:** $\Delta \vec{E} = \vec{E}_{\text{out}} - \vec{E}_{\text{in}} = \dfrac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0}\hat{s}$. ✓

    **2. Potential & Normal Derivative Check:**
    - At $r = R$: $V_{\text{out}}(R) = -\dfrac{R\sigma}{\epsilon_0}\ln(1) = 0 = V_{\text{in}}(R)$ (continuous). ✓
    - Radial derivatives at $r = R$:
      $$
      \left.\frac{\partial V_{\text{out}}}{\partial r}\right|_{r=R} = -\frac{R\sigma}{\epsilon_0 r}\bigg|_{r=R} = -\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0}, \qquad \left.\frac{\partial V_{\text{in}}}{\partial r}\right|_{r=R} = 0
      $$
      Hence $\dfrac{\partial V_{\text{out}}}{\partial r} - \dfrac{\partial V_{\text{in}}}{\partial r} = -\dfrac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0}$. ✓
  </div>
</ProblemCard>

### 3. Work & Electrostatic Energy

<div class="ed-theory-box">

**Short Theory (Notes Ch. 2 — Electrostatic Energy):**

Potential is the work per unit charge required to assemble a charge configuration:

$$
W = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^n q_i V(\vec{r}_i) = \frac{1}{2}\int \rho V\,d\tau = \frac{\epsilon_0}{2}\left(\int_{\mathcal{V}} E^2\,d\tau + \oint_{\mathcal{S}} V\vec{E}\cdot d\vec{a}\right)
$$

Taking the volume $\mathcal{V}$ over all space lets the surface integral vanish:

$$
W = \frac{\epsilon_0}{2}\int_{\text{all space}} E^2\,d\tau
$$

</div>

<ProblemCard num="2.34" title="Energy of a Uniformly Charged Solid Sphere (Three Ways)" tag="Griffiths Ch. 2 · Problem 2.34">
  <div slot="problem">
    Find the energy stored in a uniformly charged solid sphere of radius $R$ and total charge $q$ using three different methods:
    1. $W = \dfrac{1}{2}\int \rho V\,d\tau$, given $V(r) = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{q}{2R}\left(3 - \dfrac{r^2}{R^2}\right)$ for $r \le R$.
    2. $W = \dfrac{\epsilon_0}{2}\int_{\text{all space}} E^2\,d\tau$.
    3. $W = \dfrac{\epsilon_0}{2}\left(\int_{\mathcal{V}} E^2\,d\tau + \oint_{\mathcal{S}} V\vec{E}\cdot d\vec{a}\right)$ using a spherical volume $\mathcal{V}$ of radius $a > R$, and check the limit $a \to \infty$.
  </div>

  <div slot="answer">
    All three methods give $W = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left(\dfrac{3}{5}\dfrac{q^2}{R}\right)$. In part (c), the surface term $\dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{q^2}{2a} \to 0$ as $a \to \infty$.
  </div>

  <div slot="solution">
    **Method (a):** With $\rho = \dfrac{q}{\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3}$ and $d\tau = 4\pi r^2\,dr$:

    $$
    W = \frac{1}{2}\rho\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q}{2R}\int_0^R \left(3 - \frac{r^2}{R^2}\right)4\pi r^2\,dr = \frac{q\rho}{4\epsilon_0 R}\left[r^3 - \frac{r^5}{5R^2}\right]_0^R = \frac{q\rho}{5\epsilon_0}R^2 = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left(\frac{3}{5}\frac{q^2}{R}\right)
    $$

    **Method (b):** Using $\vec{E} = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{q}{R^3}r\hat{r}$ ($r < R$) and $\vec{E} = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{q}{r^2}\hat{r}$ ($r > R$):

    $$
    W = \frac{\epsilon_0}{2}\frac{q^2}{(4\pi\epsilon_0)^2}\left[\int_0^R \left(\frac{r}{R^3}\right)^2 4\pi r^2\,dr + \int_R^\infty \frac{1}{r^4}4\pi r^2\,dr\right] = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q^2}{2}\left(\frac{1}{5R} + \frac{1}{R}\right) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left(\frac{3}{5}\frac{q^2}{R}\right)
    $$

    **Method (c):** For a sphere of radius $a > R$, where $V(a) = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{q}{a}$ and $E(a) = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{q}{a^2}$:

    $$
    W = \frac{\epsilon_0}{2}\left[\frac{q^2}{(4\pi\epsilon_0)^2}\frac{4\pi}{a} + \frac{q^2}{(4\pi\epsilon_0)^2}\frac{4\pi}{5R} + \frac{q^2}{(4\pi\epsilon_0)^2}4\pi\left(-\frac{1}{r}\right)_R^a\right] = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q^2}{2}\left(\frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{5R} - \frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{R}\right) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left(\frac{3}{5}\frac{q^2}{R}\right)
    $$

    As $a \to \infty$, the surface integral $\dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{q^2}{2a} \to 0$ and the volume integral carries all the energy.
  </div>
</ProblemCard>

### 4. Uniqueness Theorems & Method of Images

<div class="ed-theory-box">

**Short Theory (Notes Ch. 3 — Potentials & Uniqueness):**

- **First Uniqueness Theorem:** In a volume $\mathcal{V}$ with charge density $\rho$, $V$ is uniquely determined if $V$ is specified on the boundary surface $\mathcal{S}$.
- **Second Uniqueness Theorem:** In a volume $\mathcal{V}$ surrounded by conductors with $\rho$ specified between them, $\vec{E}$ is uniquely determined if the total charge $Q_i$ on each conductor is given.
- **Method of Images:** Replace the conductor by image charges outside $\mathcal{V}$ that enforce the same boundary conditions on $\mathcal{S}$ — Uniqueness guarantees the exact potential inside $\mathcal{V}$.

</div>

<ProblemCard num="3.7" title="Force on +q Above a Grounded Plane with Multiple Charges" tag="Griffiths Ch. 3 · Problem 3.7">
  <div slot="problem">
    Two point charges are located on the $z$-axis above an infinite grounded conducting $xy$-plane ($V = 0$ at $z = 0$): a charge $-2q$ at $z = d$, and a charge $+q$ at $z = 3d$. Find the net force $\vec{F}$ on the charge $+q$.
  </div>

  <div slot="answer">
    $$
    \vec{F} = -\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left(\frac{29 q^2}{72 d^2}\right)\hat{z}
    $$
  </div>

  <div slot="solution">
    To satisfy $V = 0$ on the entire $z = 0$ plane, place two image charges below the plane:
    - $+2q$ at $z = -d$ (image of $-2q$ at $+d$)
    - $-q$ at $z = -3d$ (image of $+q$ at $+3d$)

    Now compute the force on $+q$ (located at $z = +3d$) due to the other three charges:
    1. From $-2q$ at $z = +d$ (distance $2d$, downward): $-\dfrac{2q}{ (2d)^2 }\hat{z}$
    2. From image $+2q$ at $z = -d$ (distance $4d$, upward): $+\dfrac{2q}{(4d)^2}\hat{z}$
    3. From image $-q$ at $z = -3d$ (distance $6d$, downward): $-\dfrac{q}{(6d)^2}\hat{z}$

    Summing all three contributions:

    $$
    \vec{F} = \frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left[\frac{-2q}{(2d)^2} + \frac{2q}{(4d)^2} + \frac{-q}{(6d)^2}\right]\hat{z} = \frac{q^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 d^2}\left(-\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{8} - \frac{1}{36}\right)\hat{z} = -\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left(\frac{29 q^2}{72 d^2}\right)\hat{z}
    $$
  </div>
</ProblemCard>

### 5. Separation of Variables (Cartesian)

<div class="ed-theory-box">

**Short Theory (Notes Ch. 3 — Separation of Variables):**

To solve Laplace's equation $\frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial y^2} = 0$ in a 2D slot ($y \in [0, a]$, $x \ge 0$) with $V(x,0) = V(x,a) = 0$, $V(\infty,y) \to 0$, and $V(0,y) = V_0(y)$, separate variables $V(x,y) = X(x)Y(y)$:

$$
V(x, y) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty C_n\,e^{-n\pi x/a}\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right), \qquad C_n = \frac{2}{a}\int_0^a V_0(y)\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right)dy
$$

</div>

<EdDiagram
  name="separation-slot"
  caption="Semi-infinite 2D slot with grounded plates at y = 0, a and specified potential V₀(y) at x = 0 (from notes p. 10)."
/>

<ProblemCard num="3.15" title="Potential Inside a Finite Rectangular Pipe" tag="Griffiths Ch. 3 · Problem 3.15">
  <div slot="problem">
    A rectangular pipe, running parallel to the $z$-axis (from $-\infty$ to $+\infty$), has three grounded metal sides at $y = 0$, $y = a$, and $x = 0$. The fourth side, at $x = b$, is maintained at a specified potential $V_0(y)$.
    1. Develop a general formula for $V(x, y)$ inside the pipe.
    2. Find $V(x, y)$ explicitly for the case $V_0(y) = V_0$ (a constant).
  </div>

  <div slot="answer">
    1. $V(x, y) = \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty C_n \sinh\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{a}\right)\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right)$, where $C_n = \dfrac{2}{a\sinh(n\pi b/a)}\displaystyle\int_0^a V_0(y)\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right)dy$.
    2. For $V_0(y) = V_0$:
    $$
    V(x, y) = \frac{4V_0}{\pi} \sum_{n=1,3,5,\dots}^\infty \frac{\sinh(n\pi x/a)\sin(n\pi y/a)}{n\sinh(n\pi b/a)}
    $$
  </div>

  <div slot="solution">
    **1. General Formula:** Separation of variables gives $V(x, y) = (Ae^{kx} + Be^{-kx})(C\sin ky + D\cos ky)$.
    - Condition (i) $V(x,0) = 0 \implies D = 0$.
    - Condition (iii) $V(0,y) = 0 \implies B = -A$, so $Ae^{kx} - Ae^{-kx} = 2A\sinh(kx)$.
    - Condition (ii) $V(x,a) = 0 \implies k = n\pi/a$ for $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$

    Combining into a general linear superposition (absorbing $2AC$ into $C_n$):

    $$
    V(x, y) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty C_n \sinh\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{a}\right)\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right)
    $$

    Apply condition (iv) at $x = b$ and use Fourier sine orthogonality:

    $$
    C_n \sinh\!\left(\frac{n\pi b}{a}\right) = \frac{2}{a}\int_0^a V_0(y)\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right)dy \implies C_n = \frac{2}{a\sinh(n\pi b/a)}\int_0^a V_0(y)\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right)dy
    $$

    **2. Constant $V_0(y) = V_0$:**

    $$
    C_n = \frac{2V_0}{a\sinh(n\pi b/a)}\int_0^a \sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right)dy = \frac{2V_0}{a\sinh(n\pi b/a)} \times \begin{cases} 0, & n \text{ even} \\ \dfrac{2a}{n\pi}, & n \text{ odd} \end{cases}
    $$

    Substituting $C_n$ back yields:

    $$
    V(x, y) = \frac{4V_0}{\pi} \sum_{n=1,3,5,\dots}^\infty \frac{\sinh(n\pi x/a)\sin(n\pi y/a)}{n\sinh(n\pi b/a)}
    $$
  </div>
</ProblemCard>

### 6. Multipole Expansion

<div class="ed-theory-box">

**Short Theory (Notes Ch. 3 — Multipole Expansion):**

Expanding $\frac{1}{\mathscr{r}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{r^2 + (r')^2 - 2rr'\cos\alpha}}$ in powers of $r'/r$ gives the systematic multipole series for any localized charge distribution:

$$
V(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{r^{n+1}} \int (r')^n P_n(\cos\alpha)\,\rho(\vec{r}')\,d\tau'
$$

- $n = 0$ (Monopole $\sim 1/r$), $n = 1$ (Dipole $\sim 1/r^2$), $n = 2$ (Quadrupole $\sim 1/r^3$).

</div>

<EdDiagram
  name="multipole-geometry"
  caption="Multipole geometry: source point r', field point r, separation |r − r'|, and angle α (from notes p. 14)."
/>

<ProblemCard num="3.28" title="Multipole Expansion of a Uniformly Charged Ring" tag="Griffiths Ch. 3 · Problem 3.28">
  <div slot="problem">
    A circular ring in the $xy$-plane (radius $R$, centered at the origin) carries a uniform line charge $\lambda$. Find the first three terms ($n = 0, 1, 2$) in the multipole expansion for $V(r, \theta)$.
  </div>

  <div slot="answer">
    $$
    V_0 = \frac{\lambda}{2\epsilon_0}\frac{R}{r}, \qquad V_1 = 0, \qquad V_2 = -\frac{\lambda}{8\epsilon_0}\frac{R^3}{r^3}(3\cos^2\theta - 1) = -\frac{\lambda}{4\epsilon_0}\frac{R^3}{r^3}P_2(\cos\theta)
    $$
  </div>

  <div slot="solution">
    Set $\vec{r} = r\sin\theta\cos\phi\,\hat{x} + r\sin\theta\sin\phi\,\hat{y} + r\cos\theta\,\hat{z}$ and $\vec{r}' = R\cos\phi'\,\hat{x} + R\sin\phi'\,\hat{y}$, with $dq = \lambda R\,d\phi'$. Then:

    $$
    \cos\alpha = \frac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{r}'}{r R} = \sin\theta(\cos\phi\cos\phi' + \sin\phi\sin\phi')
    $$

    - **$n = 0$ (Monopole):**
      $$
      \int \rho\,d\tau' = \lambda R\int_0^{2\pi} d\phi' = 2\pi R\lambda \implies V_0 = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{2\pi R\lambda}{r} = \frac{\lambda}{2\epsilon_0}\frac{R}{r}
      $$
    - **$n = 1$ (Dipole):**
      $$
      \int r'\cos\alpha\,dq = \lambda R^2\sin\theta \int_0^{2\pi} (\cos\phi\cos\phi' + \sin\phi\sin\phi')\,d\phi' = 0 \implies V_1 = 0
      $$
    - **$n = 2$ (Quadrupole):** Using $P_2(\cos\alpha) = \frac{1}{2}(3\cos^2\alpha - 1)$ and $\int_0^{2\pi} \cos^2\phi'\,d\phi' = \int_0^{2\pi}\sin^2\phi'\,d\phi' = \pi$:
      $$
      \int (r')^2 P_2(\cos\alpha)\,dq = \frac{\lambda R^3}{2}\left[3\sin^2\theta(\pi\cos^2\phi + \pi\sin^2\phi) - 2\pi\right] = \frac{\pi\lambda R^3}{2}(3\sin^2\theta - 2) = -\pi\lambda R^3 P_2(\cos\theta)
      $$
      Hence:
      $$
      V_2 = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{-\pi\lambda R^3 P_2(\cos\theta)}{r^3} = -\frac{\lambda}{4\epsilon_0}\frac{R^3}{r^3}P_2(\cos\theta) = -\frac{\lambda}{8\epsilon_0}\frac{R^3}{r^3}(3\cos^2\theta - 1)
      $$
  </div>
</ProblemCard>
