### 3.3 Separation of Variables

<div class="ed-theory-box">

**Core Theory (Griffiths §3.3 — Cartesian &amp; Spherical Separation of Variables, Completeness, Orthogonality):**

- **Separation in Cartesian Coordinates (2D &amp; 3D):** To solve Laplace's equation $\nabla^2 V = 0$ subject to Dirichlet boundary conditions on planar surfaces, adopt the product ansatz $V(x,y) = X(x)Y(y)$ (or $X(x)Y(y)Z(z)$ in 3D). Substituting into $\nabla^2 V = 0$ and dividing by $V$ isolates each coordinate into an independent term:

$$
\frac{1}{X}\frac{d^2 X}{dx^2} + \frac{1}{Y}\frac{d^2 Y}{dy^2} = 0 \implies \frac{1}{X}\frac{d^2 X}{dx^2} = C_1, \quad \frac{1}{Y}\frac{d^2 Y}{dy^2} = C_2, \qquad C_1 + C_2 = 0
$$

  Because $x$ and $y$ vary independently, each term must be a constant. The **signs of the separation constants** are fixed by the boundary conditions: directions with homogeneous ($V = 0$) conditions at two finite endpoints require **sinusoidal** solutions ($C < 0$), whereas directions extending to infinity or carrying an inhomogeneous boundary profile require **exponential/hyperbolic** solutions ($C > 0$). For the grounded slot ($y = 0, a$ grounded; $x \to \infty$ vanishing), $C_1 = +k^2$ and $C_2 = -k^2$ give $X(x) = e^{-kx}$ and $Y(y) = \sin(ky)$ with $k = n\pi/a$ ($n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$). *(Note: $k = 0$ can appear in other geometries such as cylindrical symmetry or Neumann boundaries; negative $k$ merely shuffles existing constants.)*

- **Linearity, Completeness &amp; Orthogonality ("Fourier's Trick"):** A single product mode rarely matches an arbitrary boundary function $V_0(y)$. Because $\nabla^2$ is linear, we superpose all allowed modes:

$$
V(x,y) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} C_n e^{-n\pi x/a}\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right), \qquad V(0,y) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} C_n \sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right) = V_0(y)
$$

  **Dirichlet's theorem** guarantees that $\{\sin(n\pi y/a)\}_{n=1}^\infty$ is **complete** on $0 \le y \le a$ (any piecewise-smooth $V_0(y)$ with finitely many discontinuities can be expanded). Using **orthogonality** on $[0,a]$,

$$
\int_0^a \sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right)\sin\!\left(\frac{n'\pi y}{a}\right) dy = \frac{a}{2}\delta_{nn'} \implies C_n = \frac{2}{a}\int_0^a V_0(y)\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right) dy
$$

  In 3D (e.g., an infinite rectangular pipe with sides $a, b$ along $y, z$), two separation constants give $k = n\pi/a$, $l = m\pi/b$, and an $x$-decay rate $\pi\sqrt{(n/a)^2 + (m/b)^2}$, with double-series coefficients $C_{n,m} = \frac{4}{ab}\int_0^a\int_0^b V_0(y,z)\sin(n\pi y/a)\sin(m\pi z/b)\,dy\,dz$. For a constant strip potential $V_0(y) = V_0$ in the 2D slot, the Fourier series sums explicitly to the closed form $V(x,y) = \frac{2V_0}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\!\big[\sin(\pi y/a)/\sinh(\pi x/a)\big]$.

- **Separation in Spherical Coordinates (Azimuthal Symmetry):** When $V = V(r,\theta)$ is independent of $\phi$, Laplace's equation separates via $V(r,\theta) = R(r)\Theta(\theta)$ with separation constant $l(l+1)$:

$$
\frac{d}{dr}\!\left(r^2 \frac{dR}{dr}\right) = l(l+1)R \implies R(r) = A_l r^l + \frac{B_l}{r^{l+1}}
$$

$$
\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{d}{d\theta}\!\left(\sin\theta\frac{d\Theta}{d\theta}\right) = -l(l+1)\Theta \implies \Theta(\theta) = P_l(\cos\theta)
$$

  The physical angular solutions are the **Legendre polynomials** $P_l(x)$ ($l = 0, 1, 2, \dots$), defined by **Rodrigues' formula** and normalized to $P_l(1) = 1$:

$$
P_l(x) = \frac{1}{2^l l!}\frac{d^l}{dx^l}(x^2 - 1)^l, \qquad P_0 = 1,\; P_1 = x,\; P_2 = \frac{3x^2 - 1}{2},\; P_3 = \frac{5x^3 - 3x}{2},\; P_4 = \frac{35x^4 - 30x^2 + 3}{8},\; P_5 = \frac{63x^5 - 70x^3 + 15x}{8}
$$

  *(Caveat: Each second-order angular ODE possesses a second independent solution—e.g., $\ln\tan(\theta/2)$ for $l = 0$—which diverges at $\theta = 0$ and/or $\theta = \pi$ and is discarded unless the $z$-axis is excluded from the domain.)*

- **General Azimuthally Symmetric Solution &amp; Boundary Matching:** Superposing all $l \ge 0$ gives:

$$
V(r,\theta) = \sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(A_l r^l + \frac{B_l}{r^{l+1}}\right)P_l(\cos\theta), \qquad \int_{-1}^{1} P_l(x)P_{l'}(x)\,dx = \int_0^{\pi} P_l(\cos\theta)P_{l'}(\cos\theta)\sin\theta\,d\theta = \frac{2}{2l+1}\delta_{ll'}
$$

  - *Interior of a sphere ($r \le R$):* Regularity at $r = 0$ forces $B_l = 0$; $A_l = \frac{2l+1}{2R^l}\int_0^\pi V_0(\theta)P_l(\cos\theta)\sin\theta\,d\theta$.
  - *Exterior of a sphere ($r \ge R$) with $V(\infty) = 0$:* Boundedness at $\infty$ forces $A_l = 0$; $B_l = \frac{2l+1}{2}R^{l+1}\int_0^\pi V_0(\theta)P_l(\cos\theta)\sin\theta\,d\theta$. (For a sphere in a uniform field $\vec{E}_0 = E_0\hat{z}$, keep $A_1 = -E_0$ so $V \to -E_0 r\cos\theta$ as $r \to \infty$.)
  - *Specified surface charge $\sigma_0(\theta)$ at $r = R$:* Continuity $V_{\text{in}}(R,\theta) = V_{\text{out}}(R,\theta)$ yields $B_l = A_l R^{2l+1}$, while the normal derivative jump $\left.\frac{\partial V_{\text{out}}}{\partial r} - \frac{\partial V_{\text{in}}}{\partial r}\right|_{r=R} = -\frac{\sigma_0(\theta)}{\epsilon_0}$ gives $\sum_{l=0}^\infty (2l+1)A_l R^{l-1} P_l(\cos\theta) = \frac{\sigma_0(\theta)}{\epsilon_0}$, whence $A_l = \frac{1}{2\epsilon_0 R^{l-1}}\int_0^\pi \sigma_0(\theta)P_l(\cos\theta)\sin\theta\,d\theta$. Whenever $V_0(\theta)$ or $\sigma_0(\theta)$ is a polynomial in $\cos\theta$, matching coefficients directly ("by eyeball") avoids evaluating integrals.

</div>

<ProblemCard num="3.13" title="Infinite Slot with Split Opposite-Potential Strip at x = 0" tag="Griffiths 3.13 · 2D Cartesian Separation">
  <div slot="problem">
    Find the potential in the infinite slot of Ex. 3.3 if the boundary at $x = 0$ consists of two metal strips: one, from $y = 0$ to $y = a/2$, is held at a constant potential $V_0$, and the other, from $y = a/2$ to $y = a$, is at potential $-V_0$.
  </div>

  <div slot="answer">
    $$
    V(x,y) = \frac{8V_0}{\pi}\sum_{n=2,6,10,\dots}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}e^{-n\pi x/a}\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right) = \frac{8V_0}{\pi}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-(4j+2)\pi x/a}\sin\![(4j+2)\pi y/a]}{4j+2}
    $$
  </div>

  <div slot="solution">
    From Ex. 3.3, the general separable solution satisfying $V(x,0) = 0$, $V(x,a) = 0$, and $V(\infty, y) = 0$ is (Eq. 3.30):

    $$
    V(x,y) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} C_n e^{-n\pi x/a}\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right)
    $$

    where Fourier's trick (Eq. 3.34) gives $C_n = \frac{2}{a}\int_0^a V_0(y)\sin(n\pi y/a)\,dy$. Here the boundary potential is piecewise constant:

    $$
    V_0(y) = \begin{cases} +V_0, & 0 < y < a/2, \\ -V_0, & a/2 < y < a. \end{cases}
    $$

    Evaluating the integral over the two halves:

    $$
    C_n = \frac{2V_0}{a}\left[\int_0^{a/2}\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right)dy - \int_{a/2}^{a}\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right)dy\right] = \frac{2V_0}{a}\left[\left.\frac{-\cos(n\pi y/a)}{n\pi/a}\right|_0^{a/2} + \left.\frac{\cos(n\pi y/a)}{n\pi/a}\right|_{a/2}^{a}\right]
    $$

    Simplifying the bracket:

    $$
    C_n = \frac{2V_0}{n\pi}\left[-\cos\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{2}\right) + 1 + \cos(n\pi) - \cos\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{2}\right)\right] = \frac{2V_0}{n\pi}\left[1 + (-1)^n - 2\cos\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{2}\right)\right]
    $$

    Checking the bracket $B_n = 1 + (-1)^n - 2\cos(n\pi/2)$ by parity and divisibility by $4$:
    - If $n$ is odd: $1 - 1 - 0 = 0$.
    - If $n = 4k$ (even, divisible by $4$): $1 + 1 - 2(1) = 0$.
    - If $n = 4j + 2$ ($n = 2, 6, 10, 14, \dots$): $1 + 1 - 2(-1) = 4$, so $C_n = \dfrac{8V_0}{n\pi}$.

    Substituting $C_n$ back into the series yields:

    $$
    V(x,y) = \frac{8V_0}{\pi}\sum_{n=2,6,10,\dots}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}e^{-n\pi x/a}\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right) = \frac{8V_0}{\pi}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-(4j+2)\pi x/a}\sin\![(4j+2)\pi y/a]}{4j+2}
    $$
  </div>
</ProblemCard>

<ProblemCard num="3.14" title="Surface Charge Density on the x = 0 Strip in the Infinite Slot" tag="Griffiths 3.14 · Surface Charge & Series Summation">
  <div slot="problem">
    For the infinite slot (Ex. 3.3), determine the charge density $\sigma(y)$ on the strip at $x = 0$, assuming it is a conductor at constant potential $V_0$.
  </div>

  <div slot="answer">
    $$
    \sigma(y) = \frac{2\epsilon_0 V_0}{a\sin(\pi y/a)} = \frac{4\epsilon_0 V_0}{a}\sum_{n=1,3,5,\dots}^{\infty}\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right)
    $$
  </div>

  <div slot="solution">
    The surface charge density on the conductor at $x = 0$ (whose outward normal into the interior $x > 0$ is $\hat{n} = +\hat{x}$) is given by Eq. (2.49):

    $$
    \sigma(y) = -\epsilon_0 \left.\frac{\partial V}{\partial n}\right|_{x=0} = -\epsilon_0\left.\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}\right|_{x=0}
    $$

    **Method 1 (Closed-Form Differentiation — Rigorous):** From Eq. (3.37), the Fourier series (Eq. 3.36) for the constant-potential slot sums explicitly to:

    $$
    V(x,y) = \frac{2V_0}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{\sin(\pi y/a)}{\sinh(\pi x/a)}\right)
    $$

    *(To see this directly, write $\sin(n\pi y/a) = \operatorname{Im}(e^{in\pi y/a})$ and $Z = e^{-\pi(x - iy)/a}$, so $\sum_{j=0}^\infty \frac{Z^{2j+1}}{2j+1} = \frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{1+Z}{1-Z}$, whose imaginary part is $\frac{1}{2}\arg\frac{1+Z}{1-Z} = \frac{1}{2}\tan^{-1}\!\frac{\sin(\pi y/a)}{\sinh(\pi x/a)}$.)* Differentiating with respect to $x$:

    $$
    \sigma(y) = -\epsilon_0\frac{2V_0}{\pi}\left[\frac{1}{1 + \dfrac{\sin^2(\pi y/a)}{\sinh^2(\pi x/a)}}\left(-\frac{\sin(\pi y/a)}{\sinh^2(\pi x/a)}\cdot\frac{\pi}{a}\cosh\!\left(\frac{\pi x}{a}\right)\right)\right]_{x=0}
    $$

    Simplifying before taking $x \to 0$:

    $$
    \sigma(y) = \left.\frac{2\epsilon_0 V_0}{a}\frac{\sin(\pi y/a)\cosh(\pi x/a)}{\sinh^2(\pi x/a) + \sin^2(\pi y/a)}\right|_{x=0} = \frac{2\epsilon_0 V_0}{a\sin(\pi y/a)}
    $$

    *(Note: $\sigma(y)$ diverges as $1/y$ and $1/(a-y)$ near the insulated gaps at $y = 0$ and $y = a$, as expected near a discontinuous jump from $V_0$ to $0$.)*

    **Method 2 (Formal Term-by-Term Differentiation):** Differentiating Eq. (3.36), $V(x,y) = \frac{4V_0}{\pi}\sum_{n=1,3,5,\dots}\frac{1}{n}e^{-n\pi x/a}\sin(n\pi y/a)$, term by term:

    $$
    \sigma(y) = -\epsilon_0\frac{4V_0}{\pi}\sum_{n=1,3,5,\dots}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\left(-\frac{n\pi}{a}\right)e^{-n\pi x/a}\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right)\Bigg|_{x=0} = \frac{4\epsilon_0 V_0}{a}\sum_{n=1,3,5,\dots}^{\infty}\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right)
    $$

    *(Caveat: Naive term-by-term differentiation produces a series that does not converge pointwise in the ordinary sense, though it converges in the sense of distributions / Abel summation to $\frac{2\epsilon_0 V_0}{a\sin(\pi y/a)}$; summing first via Method 1 avoids this subtlety.)*
  </div>
</ProblemCard>

<ProblemCard num="3.15" title="Rectangular Pipe with Three Grounded Sides and Specified Fourth Side" tag="Griffiths 3.15 · 2D Rectangular Pipe">
  <div slot="problem">
    A rectangular pipe, running parallel to the $z$-axis (from $-\infty$ to $+\infty$), has three grounded metal sides, at $y = 0$, $y = a$, and $x = 0$. The fourth side, at $x = b$, is maintained at a specified potential $V_0(y)$.

    1. Develop a general formula for the potential inside the pipe.
    2. Find the potential explicitly, for the case $V_0(y) = V_0$ (a constant).
  </div>

  <div slot="answer">
    1. $V(x,y) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} C_n \sinh\!\left(\dfrac{n\pi x}{a}\right)\sin\!\left(\dfrac{n\pi y}{a}\right)$, where $C_n = \dfrac{2}{a\sinh(n\pi b/a)}\int_0^a V_0(y)\sin\!\left(\dfrac{n\pi y}{a}\right)dy$.
    2. $V(x,y) = \dfrac{4V_0}{\pi}\sum_{n=1,3,5,\dots}^{\infty}\dfrac{\sinh(n\pi x/a)\sin(n\pi y/a)}{n\sinh(n\pi b/a)}$.
  </div>

  <div slot="solution">
    1. **General Formula:** Because the pipe is infinite and translationally invariant along $z$, $V = V(x,y)$ satisfies the 2D Laplace equation $\frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial y^2} = 0$ on $0 \le x \le b$, $0 \le y \le a$, with boundary conditions:
       (i) $V(x,0) = 0$, (ii) $V(x,a) = 0$, (iii) $V(0,y) = 0$, (iv) $V(b,y) = V_0(y)$.

       Choosing sinusoidal dependence in $y$ (which has homogeneous zeros at $y = 0, a$) and exponential dependence in $x$ (Eq. 3.27):

       $$
       V(x,y) = (Ae^{kx} + Be^{-kx})(C\sin ky + D\cos ky)
       $$

       Imposing (i) $V(x,0) = 0 \implies D = 0$; (iii) $V(0,y) = 0 \implies A + B = 0 \implies B = -A$, giving $e^{kx} - e^{-kx} = 2\sinh(kx)$; and (ii) $V(x,a) = 0 \implies \sin(ka) = 0 \implies k = \frac{n\pi}{a}$ ($n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$). Superposing all modes:

       $$
       V(x,y) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} C_n \sinh\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{a}\right)\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right)
       $$

       Enforcing (iv) at $x = b$ gives $\sum_{n=1}^\infty C_n\sinh(n\pi b/a)\sin(n\pi y/a) = V_0(y)$. Multiplying by $\sin(n'\pi y/a)$, integrating from $0$ to $a$, and applying orthogonality (Eq. 3.33):

       $$
       C_n\sinh\!\left(\frac{n\pi b}{a}\right) = \frac{2}{a}\int_0^a V_0(y)\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right)dy \implies C_n = \frac{2}{a\sinh(n\pi b/a)}\int_0^a V_0(y)\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right)dy
       $$

    2. **Constant Potential $V_0(y) = V_0$:** Evaluating the Fourier integral:

       $$
       C_n = \frac{2V_0}{a\sinh(n\pi b/a)}\int_0^a \sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{a}\right)dy = \frac{2V_0}{a\sinh(n\pi b/a)}\frac{a}{n\pi}\big(1 - \cos n\pi\big) = \begin{cases} 0, & n\text{ even}, \\ \dfrac{4V_0}{n\pi\sinh(n\pi b/a)}, & n\text{ odd}. \end{cases}
       $$

       Therefore:

       $$
       V(x,y) = \frac{4V_0}{\pi}\sum_{n=1,3,5,\dots}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh(n\pi x/a)\sin(n\pi y/a)}{n\sinh(n\pi b/a)}
       $$
  </div>
</ProblemCard>

<ProblemCard num="3.16" title="Cubical Box with Five Grounded Faces and Top at V₀" tag="Griffiths 3.16 · 3D Cartesian Separation">
  <div slot="problem">
    A cubical box (sides of length $a$) consists of five metal plates, which are welded together and grounded (Fig. 3.23). The top ($z = a$) is made of a separate sheet of metal, insulated from the others, and held at a constant potential $V_0$. Find the potential inside the box. *[What should the potential at the center $(a/2, a/2, a/2)$ be? Check numerically that your formula is consistent with this value.]*
  </div>

  <div slot="answer">
    $$
    V(x,y,z) = \frac{16V_0}{\pi^2}\sum_{n=1,3,5,\dots}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1,3,5,\dots}^{\infty}\frac{1}{nm}\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{a}\right)\sin\!\left(\frac{m\pi y}{a}\right)\frac{\sinh\!\left(\pi\sqrt{n^2+m^2}\,z/a\right)}{\sinh\!\left(\pi\sqrt{n^2+m^2}\right)}
    $$

    By symmetry, $V(a/2, a/2, a/2) = V_0/6$, which matches the numerical sum $S = \pi^2/96 \approx 0.102808$.
  </div>

  <div slot="solution">
    We solve $\nabla^2 V = 0$ inside $0 \le x,y,z \le a$ subject to $V = 0$ on $x=0, x=a, y=0, y=a, z=0$, and $V = V_0$ on $z = a$.

    1. **Separation of Variables:** Using $V(x,y,z) = X(x)Y(y)Z(z)$ as in Ex. 3.5, with sinusoidal behavior in $x, y$ (homogeneous on both ends) and exponential behavior in $z$:

       $$
       \frac{1}{X}\frac{d^2 X}{dx^2} = -k^2, \quad \frac{1}{Y}\frac{d^2 Y}{dy^2} = -l^2, \quad \frac{1}{Z}\frac{d^2 Z}{dz^2} = k^2 + l^2
       $$

       Imposing $V = 0$ at $x = 0, a$ and $y = 0, a$ gives $X(x) = \sin(n\pi x/a)$ and $Y(y) = \sin(m\pi y/a)$ for $n, m = 1, 2, 3, \dots$ Then $Z(z) = E e^{\sqrt{k^2+l^2}\,z} + G e^{-\sqrt{k^2+l^2}\,z}$; requiring $Z(0) = 0$ sets $G = -E$, so $Z(z) \propto \sinh\!\big(\pi\sqrt{n^2+m^2}\,z/a\big)$. Superposing all modes:

       $$
       V(x,y,z) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty} C_{n,m}\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{a}\right)\sin\!\left(\frac{m\pi y}{a}\right)\sinh\!\left(\frac{\pi\sqrt{n^2+m^2}\,z}{a}\right)
       $$

    2. **Fitting $V(x,y,a) = V_0$:** At $z = a$, this double Fourier sine series equals the constant $V_0$. By Eqs. (3.50)–(3.51):

       $$
       C_{n,m}\sinh\!\left(\pi\sqrt{n^2+m^2}\right) = \frac{4V_0}{a^2}\int_0^a\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{a}\right)dx\int_0^a\sin\!\left(\frac{m\pi y}{a}\right)dy = \begin{cases} 0, & n\text{ or }m\text{ even}, \\ \dfrac{16V_0}{\pi^2 nm}, & n,m\text{ both odd}. \end{cases}
       $$

       Hence:

       $$
       V(x,y,z) = \frac{16V_0}{\pi^2}\sum_{n=1,3,5,\dots}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1,3,5,\dots}^{\infty}\frac{1}{nm}\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{a}\right)\sin\!\left(\frac{m\pi y}{a}\right)\frac{\sinh\!\left(\pi\sqrt{n^2+m^2}\,z/a\right)}{\sinh\!\left(\pi\sqrt{n^2+m^2}\right)}
       $$

    3. **Potential at the Center &amp; Numerical Check:** Consider superposing six copies of this box, each having potential $V_0$ on one face and $0$ on the other five. Their sum has potential $V_0$ on all six faces, so by uniqueness $V_{\text{total}} = V_0$ everywhere inside—in particular at the center. By symmetry all six contribute equally at $(a/2, a/2, a/2)$, so $V(a/2, a/2, a/2) = V_0/6$.

       Evaluating the series at $(a/2, a/2, a/2)$ with $n = 2i+1$, $m = 2j+1$ ($\sin(n\pi/2) = (-1)^i$, $\sin(m\pi/2) = (-1)^j$) and the identity $\frac{\sinh u}{\sinh 2u} = \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{sech} u$:

       $$
       V\!\left(\frac{a}{2},\frac{a}{2},\frac{a}{2}\right) = \frac{16V_0}{\pi^2} S, \qquad S = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{i+j}}{(2i+1)(2j+1)}\operatorname{sech}\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{(2i+1)^2 + (2j+1)^2}\right)
       $$

       For $V = V_0/6$, we require $S = \pi^2/96 \approx 0.102808$. Summing terms up to $i, j = 3$ (the $\operatorname{sech}$ factor decays exponentially fast) yields $S \approx 0.102808$, confirming $V(a/2, a/2, a/2) = V_0/6$ to six significant digits.
  </div>
</ProblemCard>

<ProblemCard num="3.17" title="Rodrigues Formula, Differential Equation &amp; Orthogonality for P₃" tag="Griffiths 3.17 · Legendre Polynomials">
  <div slot="problem">
    Derive $P_3(x)$ from the Rodrigues formula, and check that $P_3(\cos\theta)$ satisfies the angular equation (3.60) for $l = 3$. Check that $P_3$ and $P_1$ are orthogonal by explicit integration.
  </div>

  <div slot="answer">
    $P_3(x) = \dfrac{1}{2}(5x^3 - 3x)$; satisfies $\dfrac{1}{\sin\theta}\dfrac{d}{d\theta}\!\left(\sin\theta\dfrac{dP_3}{d\theta}\right) = -12 P_3(\cos\theta)$ ($l=3$); and $\int_{-1}^{1} P_1(x)P_3(x)\,dx = 0$.
  </div>

  <div slot="solution">
    1. **Derivation of $P_3(x)$ from Rodrigues' Formula (Eq. 3.62):** For $l = 3$:

       $$
       P_3(x) = \frac{1}{2^3\,3!}\frac{d^3}{dx^3}(x^2 - 1)^3 = \frac{1}{48}\frac{d^3}{dx^3}(x^6 - 3x^4 + 3x^2 - 1)
       $$

       Taking three derivatives successively:

       $$
       \frac{d}{dx}(x^6 - 3x^4 + 3x^2 - 1) = 6x^5 - 12x^3 + 6x \implies \frac{d^2}{dx^2} = 30x^4 - 36x^2 + 6 \implies \frac{d^3}{dx^3} = 120x^3 - 72x
       $$

       Dividing by $48$:

       $$
       P_3(x) = \frac{120x^3 - 72x}{48} = \frac{1}{2}(5x^3 - 3x)
       $$

    2. **Verification of the Angular Equation (Eq. 3.60) for $l = 3$:** With $P_3(\cos\theta) = \frac{1}{2}(5\cos^3\theta - 3\cos\theta) = \frac{1}{2}\cos\theta(5\cos^2\theta - 3)$:

       $$
       \frac{dP_3}{d\theta} = \frac{1}{2}\big[-\sin\theta(5\cos^2\theta - 3) + \cos\theta(-10\cos\theta\sin\theta)\big] = -\frac{3}{2}\sin\theta(5\cos^2\theta - 1)
       $$

       Multiplying by $\sin\theta$ and differentiating with respect to $\theta$:

       $$
       \frac{d}{d\theta}\!\left(\sin\theta\frac{dP_3}{d\theta}\right) = -\frac{3}{2}\frac{d}{d\theta}\!\left[\sin^2\theta(5\cos^2\theta - 1)\right] = -\frac{3}{2}\big[2\sin\theta\cos\theta(5\cos^2\theta - 1) + \sin^2\theta(-10\cos\theta\sin\theta)\big]
       $$

       Dividing by $\sin\theta$ and replacing $\sin^2\theta = 1 - \cos^2\theta$:

       $$
       \frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{d}{d\theta}\!\left(\sin\theta\frac{dP_3}{d\theta}\right) = -\frac{3}{2}\cos\theta\big[10\cos^2\theta - 2 - 10(1 - \cos^2\theta)\big] = -\frac{3}{2}\cos\theta(20\cos^2\theta - 12) = -12\cdot\frac{1}{2}(5\cos^3\theta - 3\cos\theta)

       Since $l(l+1) = 3\cdot 4 = 12$, this equals $-l(l+1)P_3(\cos\theta)$, confirming Eq. (3.60).

    3. **Explicit Orthogonality Check ($P_1$ and $P_3$):** Using $P_1(x) = x$:

       $$
       \int_{-1}^{1} P_1(x)P_3(x)\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1} (5x^4 - 3x^2)\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\Big[x^5 - x^3\Big]_{-1}^{1} = \frac{1}{2}\big[(1 - 1) - (-1 + 1)\big] = 0 \;\checkmark

<ProblemCard num="3.18" title="Uniform Potential &amp; Uniform Surface Charge on a Sphere" tag="Griffiths 3.18 · Spherical Consistency Check">
  <div slot="problem">
    1. Suppose the potential is a constant $V_0$ over the surface of the sphere. Use the results of Ex. 3.6 and Ex. 3.7 to find the potential inside and outside the sphere. *(Of course, you know the answers in advance—this is just a consistency check on the method.)*
    2. Find the potential inside and outside a spherical shell that carries a uniform surface charge $\sigma_0$, using the results of Ex. 3.9.
  </div>

  <div slot="answer">
    1. $V(r,\theta) = V_0$ for $r \le R$, and $V(r,\theta) = V_0\dfrac{R}{r}$ for $r \ge R$.
    2. $V(r,\theta) = \dfrac{R\sigma_0}{\epsilon_0} = \dfrac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0 R}$ for $r \le R$, and $V(r,\theta) = \dfrac{R^2\sigma_0}{\epsilon_0 r} = \dfrac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r}$ for $r \ge R$ (with $Q = 4\pi R^2\sigma_0$).
  </div>

  <div slot="solution">
    1. **Constant Surface Potential $V_0(\theta) = V_0$:**
       - *Inside ($r \le R$, Ex. 3.6):* From Eq. (3.69), since $P_0(\cos\theta) = 1$, orthogonality (Eq. 3.68) gives:

         $$
         A_l = \frac{2l+1}{2R^l}V_0\int_0^{\pi} P_0(\cos\theta)P_l(\cos\theta)\sin\theta\,d\theta = \frac{2l+1}{2R^l}V_0\left(\frac{2}{2l+1}\delta_{l0}\right) = \begin{cases} V_0, & l = 0, \\ 0, & l \ne 0. \end{cases}
         $$

         Thus $V(r,\theta) = A_0 r^0 P_0(\cos\theta) = V_0$ everywhere inside.

       - *Outside ($r \ge R$, Ex. 3.7):* From Eq. (3.73):

         $$
         B_l = \frac{2l+1}{2}R^{l+1}V_0\int_0^{\pi} P_0(\cos\theta)P_l(\cos\theta)\sin\theta\,d\theta = \begin{cases} RV_0, & l = 0, \\ 0, & l \ne 0. \end{cases}
         $$

         Thus $V(r,\theta) = \dfrac{B_0}{r}P_0(\cos\theta) = V_0\dfrac{R}{r}$ everywhere outside.

    2. **Uniform Surface Charge $\sigma_0(\theta) = \sigma_0$:** From Ex. 3.9 (Eq. 3.84):

       $$
       A_l = \frac{1}{2\epsilon_0 R^{l-1}}\int_0^{\pi}\sigma_0 P_l(\cos\theta)\sin\theta\,d\theta = \frac{\sigma_0}{2\epsilon_0 R^{l-1}}(2\delta_{l0}) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{R\sigma_0}{\epsilon_0}, & l = 0, \\ 0, & l \ne 0. \end{cases}
       $$

       Using $B_l = A_l R^{2l+1}$ (Eq. 3.81), the only nonzero exterior coefficient is $B_0 = A_0 R = \dfrac{R^2\sigma_0}{\epsilon_0}$. Substituting into Eqs. (3.78)–(3.79) (and noting $Q = 4\pi R^2\sigma_0$):

       $$
       V(r,\theta) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{R\sigma_0}{\epsilon_0} = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{Q}{R}, & r \le R, \\[10pt] \dfrac{R^2\sigma_0}{\epsilon_0 r} = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{Q}{r}, & r \ge R. \end{cases}
       $$

       4\cos^3\theta - 3\cos\theta = \frac{\alpha}{2}(5\cos^3\theta - 3\cos\theta) + \beta\cos\theta = \frac{5\alpha}{2}\cos^3\theta + \left(\beta - \frac{3\alpha}{2}\right)\cos\theta

       $$

       $$
       \sigma(\theta) = \frac{\epsilon_0 k}{5R}\big[-9\cos\theta + 28(5\cos^3\theta - 3\cos\theta)\big] = \frac{\epsilon_0 k}{5R}\cos\theta(140\cos^2\theta - 93)

  <div slot="problem">
    Suppose the potential $V_0(\theta)$ at the surface of a sphere is specified, and there is no charge inside or outside the sphere. Show that the charge density on the sphere is given by

    $$
    \sigma(\theta) = \frac{\epsilon_0}{2R}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(2l + 1)^2 C_l P_l(\cos\theta),
    $$

    where

    $$
    C_l = \int_0^{\pi} V_0(\theta) P_l(\cos\theta)\sin\theta\,d\theta.
    $$
  </div>

  <div slot="answer">
    Follows directly by substituting $A_l = \dfrac{2l+1}{2R^l}C_l$ (Eq. 3.69) into $\sigma(\theta) = \epsilon_0\sum_{l=0}^\infty (2l+1)A_l R^{l-1}P_l(\cos\theta)$ (Eq. 3.83).
  </div>

  <div slot="solution">
    With no volume charge inside or outside the sphere, the potential is $V_{\text{in}}(r,\theta) = \sum_{l=0}^\infty A_l r^l P_l(\cos\theta)$ inside and $V_{\text{out}}(r,\theta) = \sum_{l=0}^\infty B_l r^{-(l+1)}P_l(\cos\theta)$ outside, with $B_l = A_l R^{2l+1}$ by continuity at $r = R$.

       $$
       \sigma(\theta) = -\epsilon_0\left.\left(\frac{\partial V_{\text{out}}}{\partial r} - \frac{\partial V_{\text{in}}}{\partial r}\right)\right|_{r=R} = \epsilon_0\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(2l + 1)A_l R^{l-1}P_l(\cos\theta)

       $$

       $$
       \sigma(\theta) = \epsilon_0\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(2l + 1)\left(\frac{2l+1}{2R^l}C_l\right)R^{l-1}P_l(\cos\theta) = \frac{\epsilon_0}{2R}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(2l + 1)^2 C_l P_l(\cos\theta)

    - (i) $V(R,\theta) = \text{constant}$ (the metal sphere is an equipotential),

    $$

       V(r,\theta) = \sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{B_l}{r^{l+1}}P_l(\cos\theta) \implies V(r,0) = \sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{B_l}{r^{l+1}}

<ProblemCard num="3.23" title="Spherical Shell with Opposite Uniform Charges on Two Hemispheres" tag="Griffiths 3.23 · Spherical Charge Shell">
  <div slot="problem">
    A spherical shell of radius $R$ carries a uniform surface charge $\sigma_0$ on the "northern" hemisphere and a uniform surface charge $-\sigma_0$ on the "southern" hemisphere. Find the potential inside and outside the sphere, calculating the coefficients explicitly up to $A_6$ and $B_6$.
  </div>

  <div slot="answer">
    - $A_0 = A_2 = A_4 = A_6 = 0$, $B_0 = B_2 = B_4 = B_6 = 0$; $A_1 = \dfrac{\sigma_0}{2\epsilon_0}$, $A_3 = -\dfrac{\sigma_0}{8\epsilon_0 R^2}$, $A_5 = \dfrac{\sigma_0}{16\epsilon_0 R^4}$; $B_1 = \dfrac{\sigma_0 R^3}{2\epsilon_0}$, $B_3 = -\dfrac{\sigma_0 R^5}{8\epsilon_0}$, $B_5 = \dfrac{\sigma_0 R^7}{16\epsilon_0}$.
    - **Inside ($r \le R$):** $V(r,\theta) = \dfrac{\sigma_0 R}{2\epsilon_0}\left[\left(\dfrac{r}{R}\right)P_1(\cos\theta) - \dfrac{1}{4}\left(\dfrac{r}{R}\right)^3 P_3(\cos\theta) + \dfrac{1}{8}\left(\dfrac{r}{R}\right)^5 P_5(\cos\theta) - \cdots\right]$.

  </div>

  <div slot="solution">
    From Ex. 3.9, $V(r,\theta) = \sum_{l=0}^\infty A_l r^l P_l(\cos\theta)$ for $r \le R$ and $\sum_{l=0}^\infty B_l r^{-(l+1)} P_l(\cos\theta)$ for $r \ge R$, with $B_l = A_l R^{2l+1}$ (Eq. 3.81) and (Eq. 3.84):

    $$
    A_l = \frac{1}{2\epsilon_0 R^{l-1}}\int_0^{\pi}\sigma_0(\theta)P_l(\cos\theta)\sin\theta\,d\theta
    $$

    Substituting $\sigma_0(\theta) = +\sigma_0$ for $0 < \theta < \pi/2$ and $-\sigma_0$ for $\pi/2 < \theta < \pi$, and changing variables to $x = \cos\theta$ ($dx = -\sin\theta\,d\theta$):

    A_l = \frac{\sigma_0}{2\epsilon_0 R^{l-1}}\left[\int_0^1 P_l(x)\,dx - \int_{-1}^0 P_l(x)\,dx\right]
    $$

    $$
    A_l = \frac{\sigma_0}{2\epsilon_0 R^{l-1}}\big[1 - (-1)^l\big]\int_0^1 P_l(x)\,dx = \begin{cases} 0, & l\text{ even}, \\[6pt] \dfrac{\sigma_0}{\epsilon_0 R^{l-1}}\int_0^1 P_l(x)\,dx, & l\text{ odd}. \end{cases}

    Thus $A_0 = A_2 = A_4 = A_6 = 0$ (and $B_0 = B_2 = B_4 = B_6 = 0$). Evaluating the odd integrals using Table 3.1:
    - **$l = 1$:** $\int_0^1 x\,dx = \frac{1}{2} \implies A_1 = \dfrac{\sigma_0}{2\epsilon_0}$, $B_1 = A_1 R^3 = \dfrac{\sigma_0 R^3}{2\epsilon_0}$.
    - **$l = 3$:** $\int_0^1 \frac{1}{2}(5x^3 - 3x)\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{5}{4} - \frac{3}{2}\right) = -\frac{1}{8} \implies A_3 = -\dfrac{\sigma_0}{8\epsilon_0 R^2}$, $B_3 = A_3 R^7 = -\dfrac{\sigma_0 R^5}{8\epsilon_0}$.
    - **$l = 5$:** $\int_0^1 \frac{1}{8}(63x^5 - 70x^3 + 15x)\,dx = \frac{1}{8}\left(\frac{63}{6} - \frac{70}{4} + \frac{15}{2}\right) = \frac{1}{8}\left(\frac{21 - 35 + 15}{2}\right) = \frac{1}{16} \implies A_5 = \dfrac{\sigma_0}{16\epsilon_0 R^4}$, $B_5 = A_5 R^{11} = \dfrac{\sigma_0 R^7}{16\epsilon_0}$.

    Assembling the expansions up to $l = 6$:

    $$
    V(r,\theta) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{\sigma_0 R}{2\epsilon_0}\left[\left(\dfrac{r}{R}\right)P_1(\cos\theta) - \dfrac{1}{4}\left(\dfrac{r}{R}\right)^3 P_3(\cos\theta) + \dfrac{1}{8}\left(\dfrac{r}{R}\right)^5 P_5(\cos\theta) - \cdots\right], & r \le R, \\[12pt] \dfrac{\sigma_0 R}{2\epsilon_0}\left[\left(\dfrac{R}{r}\right)^2 P_1(\cos\theta) - \dfrac{1}{4}\left(\dfrac{R}{r}\right)^4 P_3(\cos\theta) + \dfrac{1}{8}\left(\dfrac{R}{r}\right)^6 P_5(\cos\theta) - \cdots\right], & r \ge R. \end{cases}
    $$
  </div>
</ProblemCard>

<ProblemCard num="3.24" title="Separation of Variables in Cylindrical Coordinates (z-Independent)" tag="Griffiths 3.24 · Cylindrical Separation">
  <div slot="problem">
    Solve Laplace's equation by separation of variables in cylindrical coordinates, assuming there is no dependence on $z$ (cylindrical symmetry). *[Make sure you find all solutions to the radial equation; in particular, your result must accommodate the case of an infinite line charge, for which (of course) we already know the answer.]*
  </div>

  <div slot="answer">
    $$
    V(s,\phi) = a_0 + b_0\ln s + \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big[s^k(a_k\cos k\phi + b_k\sin k\phi) + s^{-k}(c_k\cos k\phi + d_k\sin k\phi)\Big]
    $$
  </div>

  <div slot="solution">
    With $\frac{\partial V}{\partial z} = 0$, Laplace's equation in cylindrical coordinates $(s,\phi,z)$ reads:

    $$
    \frac{1}{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\!\left(s\frac{\partial V}{\partial s}\right) + \frac{1}{s^2}\frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial\phi^2} = 0
    $$

    Substitute the product ansatz $V(s,\phi) = S(s)\Phi(\phi)$, multiply by $s^2$, and divide by $V = S\Phi$:

    $$
    \frac{s}{S}\frac{d}{ds}\!\left(s\frac{dS}{ds}\right) + \frac{1}{\Phi}\frac{d^2\Phi}{d\phi^2} = 0 \implies \frac{s}{S}\frac{d}{ds}\!\left(s\frac{dS}{ds}\right) = C_1, \quad \frac{1}{\Phi}\frac{d^2\Phi}{d\phi^2} = C_2, \qquad C_1 + C_2 = 0
    $$

    1. **Angular Equation &amp; Periodicity:** Single-valuedness of $V$ requires $\Phi(\phi + 2\pi) = \Phi(\phi)$. Positive $C_2 > 0$ gives real exponentials that cannot be periodic, so we must have $C_2 = -k^2 \le 0$ ($C_1 = +k^2$):

       $$
       \frac{d^2\Phi}{d\phi^2} = -k^2\Phi \implies \Phi(\phi) = \begin{cases} A + B\phi, & k = 0, \\ A\cos(k\phi) + B\sin(k\phi), & k > 0. \end{cases}
       $$

       Periodicity $\Phi(\phi + 2\pi) = \Phi(\phi)$ forces $B = 0$ when $k = 0$ (since $B\phi$ is not periodic), and forces $k = 1, 2, 3, \dots$ for $k > 0$ (negative integers merely repeat positive ones).

    2. **Radial Equation ($k > 0$ vs. $k = 0$):**
       - *For $k = 1, 2, 3, \dots$:* Trying $S(s) = s^n$ in $s\frac{d}{ds}\!\left(s\frac{dS}{ds}\right) = k^2 S$ yields $n^2 s^n = k^2 s^n \implies n = \pm k$, so the general radial solution is:

         $$
         S(s) = C s^k + D s^{-k}
         $$

       - *For $k = 0$:* Setting $k = 0$ in $S = C s^k + D s^{-k}$ collapses both independent solutions into a single constant. Going back to the $k = 0$ radial ODE directly:

         $$
         \frac{d}{ds}\!\left(s\frac{dS}{ds}\right) = 0 \implies s\frac{dS}{ds} = b_0 \implies \frac{dS}{ds} = \frac{b_0}{s} \implies S(s) = a_0 + b_0\ln s
         $$

         This logarithmic solution $b_0\ln s$ is precisely the potential of an infinite uniform line charge!

    3. **General Linear Combination:** Summing over $k = 0$ and all $k \ge 1$ yields the general 2D cylindrical harmonic expansion:

       $$
       V(s,\phi) = a_0 + b_0\ln s + \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big[s^k(a_k\cos k\phi + b_k\sin k\phi) + s^{-k}(c_k\cos k\phi + d_k\sin k\phi)\Big]
       $$
  </div>
</ProblemCard>

<ProblemCard num="3.25" title="Conducting Cylinder Perpendicular to a Uniform Electric Field" tag="Griffiths 3.25 · Cylindrical Boundary Value Problem">
  <div slot="problem">
    Find the potential outside an infinitely long metal pipe, of radius $R$, placed at right angles to an otherwise uniform electric field $\vec{E}_0$. Find the surface charge induced on the pipe. *[Use your result from Prob. 3.24.]*
  </div>

  <div slot="answer">
    $$
    V(s,\phi) = -E_0\left(s - \frac{R^2}{s}\right)\cos\phi \quad (s \ge R), \qquad \sigma(\phi) = 2\epsilon_0 E_0\cos\phi
    $$
  </div>

  <div slot="solution">
    Orient the pipe along the $z$-axis and the uniform field along $+\hat{x}$ ($\vec{E}_0 = E_0\hat{x}$). Setting $V = 0$ on the metal pipe (and by symmetry on the entire $yz$-plane $x = 0$), the boundary conditions for $s \ge R$ are:
    (i) $V(R,\phi) = 0$, and (ii) $V(s,\phi) \to -E_0 x = -E_0 s\cos\phi$ as $s \gg R$.

    1. **Potential Outside the Pipe:** In the general cylindrical expansion from Prob. 3.24, condition (ii) rules out $\ln s$, $a_0$, all $s^k$ terms with $k \ge 2$, and all $\sin k\phi$ terms; only $k = 1$ with $\cos\phi$ can survive ($a_1 = -E_0$, and $c_1$ allowed since $s^{-1} \to 0$ as $s \to \infty$):

       $$
       V(s,\phi) = \left(a_1 s + \frac{c_1}{s}\right)\cos\phi
       $$

       Condition (ii) fixes $a_1 = -E_0$, and condition (i) at $s = R$ requires $a_1 R + c_1/R = 0 \implies c_1 = -a_1 R^2 = +E_0 R^2$. Therefore:

       $$
       V(s,\phi) = -E_0\left(s - \frac{R^2}{s}\right)\cos\phi \qquad (s \ge R)
       $$

    2. **Induced Surface Charge Density:** Evaluating $\sigma(\phi) = -\epsilon_0\left.\dfrac{\partial V}{\partial s}\right|_{s=R}$:

       $$
       \sigma(\phi) = -\epsilon_0\left[-E_0\left(1 + \frac{R^2}{s^2}\right)\cos\phi\right]_{s=R} = 2\epsilon_0 E_0\cos\phi
       $$

       *(Notice the factor of $2$ in $2\epsilon_0 E_0\cos\phi$ for a cylinder, compared with $3\epsilon_0 E_0\cos\theta$ for a sphere in Ex. 3.8.)*
  </div>
</ProblemCard>

<ProblemCard num="3.26" title="Infinite Cylinder with Surface Charge σ(φ) = a sin(5φ)" tag="Griffiths 3.26 · Cylindrical Charge Shell">
  <div slot="problem">
    Charge density $\sigma(\phi) = a\sin 5\phi$ (where $a$ is a constant) is glued over the surface of an infinite cylinder of radius $R$ (Fig. 3.25). Find the potential inside and outside the cylinder. *[Use your result from Prob. 3.24.]*
  </div>

  <div slot="answer">
    $$
    V(s,\phi) = \frac{a\sin 5\phi}{10\epsilon_0}\begin{cases} s^5/R^4, & s \le R, \\[4pt] R^6/s^5, & s \ge R. \end{cases}
    $$
  </div>

  <div slot="solution">
    Apply the general $z$-independent cylindrical expansion from Prob. 3.24 separately inside ($s \le R$) and outside ($s \ge R$) the cylinder:
    - *Inside ($s \le R$):* Regularity at $s = 0$ eliminates $\ln s$ and $s^{-k}$ terms:

      $$
      V_{\text{in}}(s,\phi) = a_0 + \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} s^k(a_k\cos k\phi + b_k\sin k\phi)
      $$

    - *Outside ($s \ge R$):* Since the net charge per unit length is $\int_0^{2\pi} a\sin(5\phi)\,R\,d\phi = 0$, the potential cannot have a $\ln s$ term (no monopole line charge), and boundedness as $s \to \infty$ eliminates $s^k$ ($k \ge 1$):

      $$
      V_{\text{out}}(s,\phi) = a_0' + \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} s^{-k}(c_k\cos k\phi + d_k\sin k\phi)
      $$

    1. **Continuity of $V$ at $s = R$ (Eq. 2.34):** Setting $V_{\text{in}}(R,\phi) = V_{\text{out}}(R,\phi)$ matches each angular mode:

       $$
       a_0 = a_0' \;(\text{choose } a_0 = a_0' = 0), \qquad c_k = R^{2k}a_k, \qquad d_k = R^{2k}b_k
       $$

    2. **Discontinuity of Normal Derivative at $s = R$ (Eq. 2.36):**

       $$
       \left.\frac{\partial V_{\text{out}}}{\partial s}\right|_{s=R} - \left.\frac{\partial V_{\text{in}}}{\partial s}\right|_{s=R} = -\frac{\sigma(\phi)}{\epsilon_0} = -\frac{a}{\epsilon_0}\sin 5\phi
       $$

       Computing the radial derivatives at $s = R$:

       $$
       \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[-\frac{k}{R^{k+1}}(c_k\cos k\phi + d_k\sin k\phi) - k R^{k-1}(a_k\cos k\phi + b_k\sin k\phi)\right] = -\frac{a}{\epsilon_0}\sin 5\phi
       $$

       By completeness/orthogonality of $\{\cos k\phi, \sin k\phi\}$, all coefficients vanish except $k = 5$ with $\sin 5\phi$ ($a_k = c_k = 0$ for all $k$, and $b_k = d_k = 0$ for $k \ne 5$). For $k = 5$, using $d_5 = R^{10} b_5$:

       $$
       \frac{5}{R^6}d_5 + 5R^4 b_5 = 10R^4 b_5 = \frac{a}{\epsilon_0} \implies b_5 = \frac{a}{10\epsilon_0 R^4}, \qquad d_5 = \frac{a R^6}{10\epsilon_0}
       $$

    Substituting $b_5$ and $d_5$ back yields:

    $$
    V(s,\phi) = \frac{a\sin 5\phi}{10\epsilon_0}\begin{cases} s^5/R^4, & s \le R, \\[6pt] R^6/s^5, & s \ge R. \end{cases}
    $$
  </div>
</ProblemCard>
